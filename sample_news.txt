Global Climate Summit Reaches Historic Agreement on Carbon Emissions

LONDON (Reuters) - World leaders from 195 countries reached a landmark agreement at the Global Climate Summit on Friday, pledging to reduce carbon emissions by 55 percent by the year 2035. The deal, which was finalized after two weeks of intense negotiations in Geneva, represents the most ambitious international climate commitment in modern history.

The agreement includes binding targets for both developed and developing nations, with a specialized fund of 100 billion dollars annually to assist poorer countries in transitioning to renewable energy sources. "This is not just a piece of paper. This is a promise to future generations that we will not abandon them to an unlivable planet," said United Nations Secretary-General Maria Santos during the closing ceremony.

Environmental groups have cautiously welcomed the agreement, though some activists argue that the timeline remains too slow given the accelerating pace of climate change. Scientists have warned that global temperatures could rise by more than 2.5 degrees Celsius above pre-industrial levels by 2050 without immediate and drastic action.

The agreement also addresses methane emissions, deforestation, and ocean acidification, marking the first time these issues have been included in a comprehensive global climate framework. Financial markets reacted positively to the news, with clean energy stocks surging across major exchanges in New York, Tokyo, and Frankfurt.

Implementation of the agreement will begin in January next year, with each country required to submit detailed national action plans within six months. An independent international monitoring body will be established to track progress and ensure compliance with the agreed targets.
